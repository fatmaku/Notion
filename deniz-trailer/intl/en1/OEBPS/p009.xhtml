Adventure 3

The Itchy Tag Monster

On Tuesday morning, there was a war at home. The reason: a new sweater.

“Come on, put it on,” said his dad. “Look how lovely and soft it is.”

But Deniz knew better. Right in the collar of that sweater sat a TAG MONSTER. A teeny-tiny monster with pointy teeth, and oh, how it itched. Nobody could see it — but Deniz’s super skin could feel it. Scritch, scritch, scritch!

“It’s itchy!” said Deniz.

“There’s nothing there,” said his dad.

Pusula landed on Dad’s shoulder (Dad had no idea, of course) and winked at Deniz: “Show your dad the scissors.”

Deniz fetched the scissors. “Dad, if we cut the tag out, the monster goes away. I promise I’ll wear it then.”

His dad raised one eyebrow… then he laughed and cut the tag out. Deniz put the sweater on. The monster was gone!

Pusula fluttered her wings. “Did you notice?” she said. “You fetched the scissors. That was a sentence too.”

“But I talked,” said Deniz.

“You did. Some children tell you with words. Some don’t speak at all — and they still tell you: they show a picture, hand you a card, point with a finger, take your hand and lead you there. A fetched pair of scissors, an offered card, a picture pointed at … those are all sentences. What matters isn’t that it comes out of your mouth — it’s that the person with you understands.”

That evening, Deniz was sad; it had been a hard day at school. He asked his dad, “Daddy, will you hug me TIGHT? But tell me first.”

His dad was surprised at first — a light touch always bothered Deniz, but a tight hug… He tried it. A great big, tight, long hug. The whole storm inside Deniz slowed down. “You’re my charger, Daddy,” said Deniz.

🦋 Pusula saysYour skin is super: it feels even the tiniest things. Cutting out tags is nothing to be ashamed of! And here’s a secret: a light touch tickles, but a TIGHT hug charges you up. Ask people to warn you first — “Hug coming!”
💜 For Parents and TeachersDon’t say “There’s nothing there” — on his skin, there is; in these children, the getting-used-to-it signal never arrives. Cut out the tags, buy seamless socks. And know the paradox: an unannounced light touch is an alarm, expected deep pressure is medicine. Asking “Can I give you a hug?” takes two seconds — and turns touch into medicine.
