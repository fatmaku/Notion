Adventure 5

The Traffic Light in Your Tummy

On Wednesday afternoon, Deniz suddenly got very, VERY angry. He cried because his Lego broke, he yelled at his little brother, he slammed the door. Then he surprised even himself: “Why did I do that?”

Pusula came and pointed a wing at Deniz’s tummy: “There’s a traffic light in your tummy, but its bulb is turned way down low. The hungry light, the thirsty light, the tired light… They’re glowing, but you can’t see them. Then all of them turn BRIGHT RED at once and — BOOM!”

“So… I was hungry?!”

“Try it and see. Go to the kitchen.”

Deniz ate a banana and drank a big glass of water. And… like magic: the world fixed itself. The Lego looked fixable. His little brother wasn’t so annoying after all.

“WOW,” said Deniz. “I wasn’t angry. I was hungry!”

From that day on, Deniz’s family had a game: whenever Deniz got grumpy, they asked three questions first — Water? Food? Break? Most of the time, the answer was one of those three.

🦋 Pusula saysWhen you feel very angry, try this first: drink a glass of water, eat a little snack, rest for a bit. Sometimes anger is really hunger — wearing a costume!
💜 For Parents and TeachersInner signals (hunger, thirst, needing the toilet, tiredness) come through faintly in these children — it’s called “interoception.” An explosion “out of nowhere” is usually an inner announcement arriving late. Instead of “What’s wrong?”, run the checklist: water → snack → toilet → quiet corner. Half of all crises get solved at the kitchen table.
