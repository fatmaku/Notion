Adventure 4

The Food Fortress and the Ketchup Rule

Deniz’s plate has rules. Rule one: the ketchup does NOT touch the sausage. Rule two: the rice stays on its own side. Rule three: if everything is the same every day, everything is all right.

When his aunt came to visit, she didn’t know the rules. “Just try one bite,” she said. “You’ll starve!”

Deniz looked at the plate. The food looked soft and slimy. His mouth was saying “NO!” before it even got close.

Pusula whispered from behind the fruit bowl: “Deniz, did you know there’s super skin inside your mouth, too? The feel of some foods sets off its alarm. You’re not being naughty — your mouth is like a guard at the gate: it won’t let in anyone it doesn’t know.”

“Will it always be like this?”

“No! The guard just needs time to meet the new food. First, let it sit at the edge of your plate — just sit there. Tomorrow, you give it a sniff. The day after, maybe a tiny little touch. No hurry at all.”

Deniz told his mom. His mom smiled at the aunt: “Deniz has his safe foods; the new ones come as guests, little by little.” And nobody made Deniz eat. And do you know the funny part? Two weeks later, Deniz tried that “slimy” food all by himself. Just a fingertip’s worth. Not bad at all!

🦋 Pusula saysLoving the same food every day is nothing to be ashamed of — that’s your safe food. Let a new food sit at the EDGE of your plate first, like a guest. You can’t force a friendship — not with people, and not with food.
💜 For Parents and TeachersPicky eating is usually about texture, not taste — the inside of the mouth is “super skin” too. “He’ll eat when he’s hungry enough” doesn’t work with these children: hungry + alarm = panic. Keep the safe foods steady and let new foods be pressure-free “guests at the edge”: sitting → sniffing → touching → maybe tasting. It can take weeks; that is the way.
