SOURCES AND FURTHER READING

Where can I get support?

This is a storybook; it does not diagnose or recommend treatment. The organizations below are starting points for parents and teachers looking for reliable, up-to-date information.

  • World Health Organization (WHO) — ICD-11 — ICD-11, the International Classification of Diseases; it contains the diagnostic categories used for neurodevelopmental conditions. who.int
  • American Psychiatric Association (APA) — DSM-5-TR — Reference for diagnostic criteria. psychiatry.org
  • American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) — Family guidance on child development and behavior. healthychildren.org
  • NICE (UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence) — Clinical guidelines on autism and ADHD. nice.org.uk
  • CDC — Learn the Signs. Act Early. — Developmental milestones and early identification. cdc.gov
  • National Autistic Society (UK) — Support, guidance and local services. autism.org.uk
  • CHADD (US) — Information and support for ADHD. chadd.org
  • Understood.org — Learning and thinking differences — for families and teachers.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (US) — Free and confidential, 24/7, for anyone in distress — call or text 988. 988lifeline.org
  • Childline (UK) — Free and confidential for children and young people — 0800 1111. childline.org.uk
  • School support: IEP / 504 plan (US), SEN support / EHC plan (UK) — Ask your school’s support team about the process where you live.
  • ISAAC — International Society for AAC — For children who speak little or not at all. isaac-online.org
💜 When should I seek professional help?

If there is pain, bleeding, fever, a persistent sleep or eating problem, a sudden loss of skills, harm to self or others, ongoing school absence, or any difficulty that clearly affects daily life — don’t wait. Contact your pediatrician, your school’s support team, or a child and adolescent mental health professional. Early support lightens the load for the child.
