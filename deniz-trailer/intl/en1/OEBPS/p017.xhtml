Adventure 7

Storm Day and the Calm Harbor

Thursday was a bad day. School was crowded, his jacket got soaked, his favorite pencil went missing, and somebody yelled very loudly on the school bus. It was as if there were a balloon inside Deniz — and with every bad thing, it puffed up a little more. It puffed, and puffed, and puffed…

And at home, when his little brother accidentally knocked over his tower — POP! The balloon burst.

Deniz cried, he screamed, he threw the pillows. The sounds were too much, the lights were too much, everything was TOO MUCH. Even his words disappeared; Deniz couldn’t talk anymore.

His mom came in. She didn’t shout. She didn’t even say “Calm down.” She dimmed the light. She took the little brother out of the room. And she said only this: “I’m here. You’re safe.”

Then she waited. Storms settle when you wait for them — his mom knew that.

Slowly, slowly, the balloon went down. Deniz snuggled up close to his mom. His mom asked, “Would you like some water? Can I hug you?” Deniz nodded. One tight hug. One glass of water. The storm was over.

Pusula was watching from the window, her wings glowing with happiness: Deniz’s mom was a calm harbor. And a ship with a calm harbor comes safely out of every storm.

🦋 Pusula saysIf the balloon inside you is puffing up, say so: “My balloon is puffing up!” Ask for a break. And if the balloon bursts — it is not your fault. The storm passes. It always passes.
💜 For Parents and TeachersA meltdown is not a tantrum; it’s a full system overflowing — it wants no audience and makes no deals. In the storm: few words (“I’m here. You’re safe.”), dim the lights, lower the noise, ask before touching, and afterwards no lecture — just water and warmth. Your calm is contagious; this is called “co-regulation.” Talk tomorrow, when everyone’s tank is full.
