Adventure 6

The Rocking Dance

When Deniz is happy, he jumps and flaps his hands like a bird. When he’s thinking, he rocks back and forth. It feels so good — the jumble inside him untangles as he rocks.

But one day at the playground, a boy laughed: “Look at him, rocking like that — how weird!”

Deniz froze. His hands slid into his pockets. He didn’t rock at all that day. And by evening he was full to the brim — a Deniz who can’t rock had been collecting a storm.

Pusula landed on the end of his bed: “I’m going to tell you a secret. Everybody rocks. Grown-ups jiggle their legs, click their pens, twirl their hair, squeeze a stress ball, crack their knuckles. Waves rock in the sea, trees rock in the wind — even the Earth spins! Your rocking is a lullaby your body sings to itself. Is a lullaby something to be ashamed of?”

“No…”

“No! The boy who laughed just didn’t know, that’s all. When someone doesn’t know, you teach them — you don’t feel ashamed.”

The next day at the playground, the same boy stared again. This time, Deniz didn’t stop. “This is my super charging dance,” he said. “Try it if you like — it feels amazing.” The boy was surprised at first… then he tried it. The two of them rocked together, and laughed.

🦋 Pusula saysRocking, jumping, flapping your hands — that’s your charging dance. It’s how your body calms itself down. Not shameful, not broken: SUPER.
💜 For Parents and TeachersThis is called “stimming”: the nervous system regulates itself through rhythmic movement — suppress it, and tension piles up and the storm grows. Don’t block it; if needed, find forms that fit the setting (a stress ball, ankle circles). And teach your child that one sentence: “This is my charging dance.” Naming things is the best vaccine against shame.
