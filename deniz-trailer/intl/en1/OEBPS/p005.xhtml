Adventure 1

Super Eyes and the Disco Supermarket

On Saturday, Deniz went to the supermarket with his mom. He stopped right at the door.

The lights were so bright. And they were blinking — on and off, on and off, faster than anyone could see. But Deniz could see it. The shelves were bursting with colors, there were words everywhere, and the checkouts went beep-beep-beep.

“Mom,” said Deniz, “this place is like a disco!”

His mom laughed. “A disco? Sweetheart, it’s just a plain old supermarket.”

Pusula landed on Deniz’s shoulder and whispered, “Your mom is right — for her eyes, it is plain. But your eyes are SUPER. You see everything — even the flicker of the lights. That’s why the supermarket makes you tired. And being tired is nothing to be ashamed of; super machines get warm when they do lots of work.”

“So what do I do?” Deniz asked on the inside. (He could talk to Pusula without making a sound — that’s another power super-brain kids have.)

“You take a break,” said Pusula. “Tell your mom, ‘My eyes are tired.’ That’s a magic sentence.”

Deniz tried it. “Mom, my eyes are tired.”

His mom stopped, looked at Deniz, and held out her hand. “Come on, let’s wait outside — Dad can finish the shopping.” Outside, it was calm. Deniz took a deep breath. Once his super eyes had rested, the world was beautiful again.

🦋 Pusula saysIf your eyes see a whole lot, it’s normal for them to get tired. Saying “My eyes are tired” is the magic sentence that comes before crying and shouting. Try it!
💜 For Parents and TeachersThis is called “sensory overload”: a brain that takes everything in without a filter genuinely works harder in places like supermarkets. Instead of “Come on, you’re fine,” take a short break — five minutes of quiet beats half an hour of meltdown. Teaching a child to say “I’m tired” is one of the most valuable skills there is.
