🦋 Pusula says

If you don’t hear your body’s message right away, it isn’t your fault. Sometimes it helps to do a quick check instead of waiting for a feeling: “Toilet? Water? Rest? Care?”

💜 For Parents and Teachers

Noticing hunger, thirst, the need for the toilet and tiredness inside your own body is called interoception. Some children feel the signal very early, others only at the last moment. Instead of “But I just asked you!”, build short check-in moments into the natural transitions of the day: before leaving the house, before arriving at school, after meals, before a long stretch of play and at bedtime.

Checking does not mean forcing a child to sit on the toilet. The aim is to build body awareness — and to separate an accident from shame. A picture sequence, the same words every time and calm repetition work better than long explanations.
