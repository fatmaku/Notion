WHAT PUSULA SAYS

The doctor listened to Deniz’s tummy and explained to the family what to do. She didn’t tell Deniz off. “This happens to a lot of children,” she said.

At home they put a little footstool in front of the toilet. Now when Deniz sat down, his feet no longer dangled in the air. His knees could bend comfortably.

After meals they had a short station time. The aim was not to poop right away. The aim was to sit calmly enough for his body to hear the train. If Deniz wanted, he looked at a picture book while he sat. When the time was up, he got up.

His mom praised him for sitting and listening to his body — not for a result.

A few days later the train rolled slowly into the station. When Deniz felt the relief, Pusula beat her wings.

“You didn’t pull the train by force,” she said. “You made the tracks safe.”
