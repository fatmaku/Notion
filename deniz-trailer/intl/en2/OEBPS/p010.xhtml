ADVENTURE 2

The Poo Train and the Waiting Station

For three days Deniz had not wanted to poop.

It wasn’t really not wanting to. Last time it had hurt. Now there was a train in his tummy, and every time that train came near the station, Deniz shut the gate.

“I’m not coming out,” he thought. “If I come out, it will hurt again.”

But the longer the train waited, the heavier the carriages became. Deniz’s tummy started to ache and he went off his food. In the middle of playing he would suddenly go up on tiptoes, squeeze his legs together and then say, “It’s nothing.”

Pusula settled on the towel in the bathroom.

“Why do you keep sending the poop train back?” she asked.

Deniz lowered his head. “Because the tracks hurt.”

This time Pusula didn’t make a joke. “Then we won’t solve this on our own. If something hurts, we tell a grown-up. Your body isn’t asking for a punishment — it’s asking for help.”

Deniz went to his mom. It was hard to say, but he used the short sentence:

“Pooping hurts me.”

His mom took him seriously right away. “Thank you for telling me,” she said. “We’ll talk to our doctor.”
