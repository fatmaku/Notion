GETTING TO KNOW

The Body Post Office

That morning Deniz stopped halfway through putting on his socks.

“Pusula,” he said, “when my body wants to tell me something, why does it sometimes whisper and sometimes suddenly shout?”

Pusula flew a small loop in front of the window. Tiny envelopes drifted down from her gold-patterned wings. There was no writing on the envelopes. One had a drop of water on it, one a moon, one a toilet, one a toothbrush.

“Because your body is a post office,” said Pusula. “Every organ sends you letters. The bladder says, ‘I’m filling up.’ The bowel says, ‘I’m ready.’ The teeth say, ‘Clean me.’ The brain says, ‘Let’s go to sleep.’ But your mailman sometimes runs very fast — and sometimes he delivers the letter late.”

Deniz held an envelope to his ear. Not a sound came out.

“And what if the letter never comes?” asked Deniz.

“Then we don’t wait around feeling alone,” said Pusula. “We check. Clocks, pictures, orders and small routines are the helpers of the body post office.”

Deniz smiled. “So if I don’t understand my body, I’m not a bad child?”

Pusula’s wings lit up.

“No. You’re only just learning the map of the post office.”
