A Note for Grown-Ups

When a child cries because the plan has changed, cannot come away from a screen, or tears up the paper over one small mistake, it is not always about getting his own way. His brain may be struggling to build the crossing, to carry the not-knowing, or to slow the speed of the feeling down.

This book holds three things at the same time:

  • The feeling is real.
  • The boundary can still be real.
  • The child needs a bridge between the feeling and the boundary.

A compass for grown-ups: In the moment of overflow — few words, safety and settling. In the calm moment — teaching and planning together.

If there is long-lasting, intense anxiety, a risk of harm to himself or others, a clear loss of everyday functioning, or a sudden change in behavior, ask for proper support. Speak to your pediatrician or the school counselor and student support team. For a child or a parent who needs to talk something through confidentially: in the US, the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988); in the UK, Childline is free on 0800 1111.
