This book is not a medical resource. It does not diagnose, does not recommend treatment, and is not a substitute for professional assessment. Deniz’s adventures are inspired by the everyday lives of neurodivergent children and do not describe any particular child. If you have concerns about your child, speak to your paediatrician, a child and adolescent mental health professional, a psychologist, or your school’s support team.

Not every child tells you the same way. Deniz speaks in this book. But some children speak little or not at all — and they still tell you: they show pictures, hand over cards, point, take your hand, or use a tablet or an AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) device. All of these are valid sentences. If your child doesn’t speak the way Deniz does, translate the story into their way: “How would you show me?” — and accept that the answer may be silence.

The purple boxes: The purple box at the end of each adventure is the For Parents and Teachers; in a few sentences it explains to parents and teachers what is going on behind the scenes.

The adventures in this book don’t tell you about big solutions. They tell you this: small adjustments matter enormously. Moving to the front row, cutting out a tag, allowing headphones, or giving five minutes’ warning — these look small, but they change a child’s whole day.
