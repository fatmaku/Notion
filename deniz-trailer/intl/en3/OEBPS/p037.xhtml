💜 For Parents and Teachers

Do not tell a child “just ignore it”, “hit them back” or “you should have stood up for yourself”. Listen first, make them safe, write down the concrete details, and follow the school’s process through. Do not treat one conversation as the end of it; ask the child again over the following days.

If the child finds it hard to tell you what happened, use drawings, a body map, place-and-time cards or multiple-choice questions. In immediate danger, take the child out of the situation and set the setting’s safeguarding procedure in motion. Ask the school for its anti-bullying policy in writing. Free confidential help is also available: in the US, the Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline (call 1-800-422-4453), plus the guidance at StopBullying.gov; in the UK, Childline on 0800 1111.
