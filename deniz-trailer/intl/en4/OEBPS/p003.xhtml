The Invisible Speech Bubbles

Deniz was watching two children in the park.

One of them said, “You are such a potato!”

The other one roared with laughter.

Deniz was puzzled. Nobody looked anything like a potato.

Then a child said, “Come over if you want.” But he turned his face the other way.

When Deniz came closer, the game stopped.

“Did he really want me to come?” he thought. “Or didn’t he?”

The words that came out of people’s mouths sometimes went in a straight line, sometimes curved, and sometimes carried a meaning you couldn’t see at all.

Pusula landed on Deniz’s shoulder.

“Does the social world feel like a foggy forest?” she asked.

Deniz nodded. “Everyone else has a map. I haven’t.”

“The map may be missing,” said Pusula. “But you have your compass: ask, check, set your boundary — and listen to the other person too.”
