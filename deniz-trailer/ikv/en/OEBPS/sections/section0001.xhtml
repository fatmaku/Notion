A Special Note

I wrote this book for everyone who has supported me on this path, for those who wish to understand, for all my readers, and above all for my neurodivergent friends.

To my daughters: I walked this path so that you would never feel my absence as a kind of invisibility. I am not afraid of death. I am not particularly brave, either. It is only that my ache runs very deep. And one thing you should know from the start: this book tells perhaps only ten percent of the traumas I have lived through. The rest I have held back — to protect myself, but above all to protect my family, my children, and my bond with them. Everything written here is true; yet what is left unsaid is far more.

Foreword

This is not a success story. This is the story of a person who wanted to understand himself — someone who broke into a thousand pieces along the way and put himself back together.

If you are reading this book… you are either searching for yourself or you have lost yourself. They are the same place.

I will not show you a path. But I will show you that you are not alone on this one. Because the person described here is me. And at the same time… it is you.

A small warning before we begin: you will find a great deal of pain in this book. But you will find a great deal of humor too. This is not a contradiction. For years, humor was the only tool that let me sit in the same room as my own mind without either of us slamming the door and walking out. So if I suddenly crack a joke in the middle of a dark scene, that is not me running away. It is the very technique that kept me alive.

There is one more thing I want you to know from the start, because it is the true voice of this book. My mind never goes quiet. While I am telling you one thing, another film is running inside my head at the very same time: "What if it had gone differently? What if I had said something else that day? What if I had stayed, what if I had left, what if I had never spoken that one sentence?" What happened is lived only once, but inside me it replays a hundred times, each time with a different ending. I keep reconstructing the past; I feel possibilities that never happened as though they had; I tie every detail to another. Some have called this "overthinking." But it is not a flaw — it is the way a neurodivergent mind works. My brain never stops making connections, never stops weighing possibilities, never stops asking. Is it exhausting? Very. But it is also the very thing that made me write this book: because I did not simply live my life — I ran it again inside myself a thousand times over.

Those Inner voice boxes you will see throughout the book are exactly that unstoppable voice. As you read them, you are hearing that constant "what if?" inside a person's head. If you carry such a mind yourself, you may see, for the first time, someone putting that voice into words. And perhaps, for the first time, you will feel this: that voice is not a malfunction. It is simply an engine working very hard.

At the end of each chapter you will find three things. The first is a calm, expert assessment — I call it the Therapist's Note: what is happening, what could happen if it goes unhealed, and what the path to healing looks like. The second is a short section titled Where I Stand Today; there I honestly answer the one question that truly matters: have I left behind what that chapter describes, or am I still carrying it? And the third is for you: Your Turn. There are lines left for you there — fill them without hesitation. This book is as much yours to write in as it is to read. Don't just read the book — talk with it.

After a certain point, this book repeats itself. On purpose. Some sentences come around again and again, some lessons meet you anew in different chapters. Because I did not learn life only once; I learned the same lesson many times, each time a little more deeply. Like a good teacher, this book gently reminds you of what you have forgotten — without your noticing. Perhaps you will pause at a sentence and say, "this is exactly me." That is the moment the book is doing its work: it is not teaching you something; it is showing you what was already inside you.

And the whole book has a single heartbeat. Let me say it from the start so that you recognize it when you hear it at the end of every chapter: I am never finished; I am becoming, each day, right up to my final breath. Understanding this is, I think, the key to everything. Because there is no such thing as a "finished," "complete," "arrived" person. There are only people who become a little more themselves each day. I am one of them. So are you.
