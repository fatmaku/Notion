What I Believe

Before this book begins, I have to tell you what I believe. Because every story here actually rests on these few sentences.

The first thing I learned was resonance. When you give too much, life answers you—even if the answer feels harsh. Because in truth there is no good or evil; that division is something we make. What truly exists is pure energy — vibration, quantum. That is why a person doesn't have to bury or erase their dark sides, but to accept and embrace them. To become whole is not to choose the light side, but to own both sides.

My most important rule is: everyone is equal in worth. Everyone has the ability to do something in a special way. Because everything is in balance: whoever hears well may see less well; whoever cannot see hears all the better. What we call a “lack” has usually become an abundance somewhere else. The scale is always even.

My second rule: everything and everyone is my mirror. The person before me stirs whatever unsettles or soothes me. Do my eyes decide what is beautiful and what is ugly? If I were blind, a steak from the supermarket would taste the same as one from a seven-star restaurant; a diamond would be just a stone. If I am blind and don't know it, someone has to tell me — because no one is born knowing that something is missing. It is the world that teaches us the lack.

I am not the healing of my parents' or my ancestors' mistakes. Everyone governs themselves. No one can steal energy from me, no one can give me energy; no natural stone can do that. What influences me are my own thoughts that believe in it — or people who can turn my thoughts toward the negative or the positive. But only if I allow it.

★ That is why I will never be “better” — only ever a better model of myself.

And if no one sees me, I do not exist. So I want to be seen, in order to exist. Yet being alone showed me exactly the opposite: a person is never truly alone — because I see myself. When I see myself, I exist.

I am a part of the source; therefore a part of the origin of everything, of that creative essence. So I am on the way. The more I see, learn and come to know; the more everything mirrors me and I grasp that everything is a part of the source, the closer I come to it. And yet I was never far from it; I only understand it now. It all makes sense. That is why I no longer move forward only as “I,” but as “we.” I love everything — because everything is a part of the source, the origin of everything.

People often ask me when I learned to speak, when I walked, whether I can hold eye contact. But that should not be the question. The real question should be: Who am I? — to get to know myself, to teach myself something, to ask for my own opinion. That would matter.

The source loves us. Hell and heaven are where we are. We are here to learn to be human, and to return to the source again. We are here to understand everything, not to survive. And that is exactly why the right answer to everything is a question.

In the Quran, the first command is “Read.” But nothing was written yet. So the first task was: read yourself, read your surroundings, read life; live, learn, and write down what you have learned — so that others can continue where you left off.

Everyone is my mirror. The “evil” person before my eye is what I perceive as evil — and at the same time he shows me the dark side within me that I fear. And what if I lose control… There — you see a question again.

I no longer judge anyone. I have never lived under the same conditions as the person I am judging. As long as I am not in the same situation, I should stay silent and judge only myself. For everything I once judged, talked about and feared, I ended up living myself — or felt that very state. What is good or evil always lies in the eye of the beholder. That is why I have decided: to accept everything, to understand, and to evaluate only myself.

I carry a few truths, a few principles. You shall not place anything beside God (no shirk). You are not more valuable than another — but no less valuable either. And you are not an intermediary: between you and God stands no one; you only illuminate — whoever is to walk, walks on their own feet. Arrogance and envy kill; both pull you away from yourself and from one another. I am you, and you are me; when we become “we,” the scale returns to balance. In the bad, you too are present; in the good as well; in the devil within me and the angel within me there is the same “I.” Everyone is in their task — the awakened and the sleeping alike. Don't be afraid to live just because you have awakened; in living there is pain, and there is taste. Don't hide: walk, accept, learn, grow — then teach, and keep growing.

This is my beginning. Come now, let us walk — not as “I,” but as “we.”

A brief note on sources: Much in this credo is my own conviction — where there is no external source, I am the source, and you may read it as my personal view. Where my thoughts touch known ideas, let me name them: when I say “everything is energy and vibration,” I mean it as an image, not a physical claim — the word “quantum” here is a metaphor for interconnectedness, not a doctrine of quantum physics. “Everyone is my mirror” touches what C. G. Jung called projection. “I am a part of the source” is a conviction I share with the mystical tradition, above all with Mevlana Rumi and Sufism. And “Read!” comes, as I said, from the Quran — Iqra (Surah 96, Al-ʿAlaq, verse 1).

I have not arrived. I am becoming — day by day — and it ends only with my last breath.

Afterword

Who am I?

I am the one who falls. I am the one who rises. I am the one who searches. I am the one who finds. But most of all: I am someone who is still walking.

The road is not yet over. Yesterday I knew who I was. Today I understand. Today I am becoming. Tomorrow I am everyone and no one. Because every pain I have known, every "click" of the door, every scent of a baby, every lonely night, every fall and every rising — all of it drew me closer to God and to myself.

This book is not a tale of success. This book is the story of a person who shattered into a thousand pieces and found his way back to himself.

If you are reading these lines, know this: You are not alone. I am here. You are here. The road may be long. It may be cold. It may be dark. But it is worth walking.

Don't give up. Keep asking the question. Even if you fall, rise again. Feel out of place, be different — just be yourself. Because you are already enough. Exactly as you are.

Thank you. Thank you for walking this road with me.

Above all to my brother Sefa: Without your support, your patience and your presence, this road would not have meant nearly as much. Thank you, my brother.

And to my daughters: If one day you read this book — your father loved you deeply. He still loves you. I walked this road so that you would never feel my absence as if you were invisible. Even as the distance grows, even as time passes — this love will never end.

If you ask me who I am: Yesterday I knew. Today I am becoming, right now. Tomorrow, I do not yet know who I will be. And perhaps that is exactly the answer.

The road goes on.
I am walking.
You walk too.
